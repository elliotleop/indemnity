# The Indemnity Ladder: How Baskets, Caps and Price Move Together in Acquisition Agreements

Elliot Past and Rory Campbell. Working draft, 27 October 2026.

**[Read the paper (PDF)](Past_Campbell_Indemnity_Ladder_2026.pdf)**

## Abstract

Private acquisition agreements limit the seller's liability for breached representations with a ladder of negotiated terms: a per-claim de minimis, a basket that losses must exceed before the seller pays, a cap on the seller's total exposure, and a survival period for claims. We ask whether these terms move together: comparing deals of the same price, does a higher cap go with a higher basket? We extract the ladder from tens of thousands of agreements filed with the SEC, check the extraction against 279 hand-marked agreements, and test hypotheses registered in advance on three held-out samples spanning 1995–2025. The basket rises with both the price and the cap, each holding the other fixed, and which one it follows depends on the industry. In manufacturing and services it follows the price: on 383 held-out deals, twice the price goes with a basket about 50% larger and twice the cap with one about 25% larger (elasticities 0.60 and 0.33 [0.20, 0.45]). In financial-sector deals, including real-estate trusts and insurers, it follows the cap: about 70% larger for twice the cap and 17% for twice the price (0.77 [0.63, 0.90] and 0.23; 81 deals). Both patterns were registered from a development sample. A single pooled elasticity, 0.51 [0.43, 0.58] on 673 deals, mixes the two. Within each group the basket tracks the cap more closely in larger deals, by about 0.1 per tenfold increase in price. Deals with representations-and-warranties insurance have far lower caps and somewhat lower baskets. The estimates cover deals whose basket and cap are both stated in dollars, about two-thirds of those in which we find both terms.
